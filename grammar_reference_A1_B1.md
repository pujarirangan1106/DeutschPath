# German Grammar Reference — Level by Level

Quick-reference for what grammar is expected at each level, plus common mistakes to watch out for. Focused on A1–B1 (your exam level), with a short outlook to B2+ and consolidated cross-level references (Tenses, Cases, Pronouns) at the end.

---

## A1 — The Basics

### Verb conjugation (Präsens)
Regular verbs: **-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en** (ich/du/er-sie-es/wir/ihr/sie-Sie)
- machen → ich mache, du machst, er macht, wir machen, ihr macht, sie machen

Irregular but essential:
- **sein** (to be): ich bin, du bist, er ist, wir sind, ihr seid, sie sind
- **haben** (to have): ich habe, du hast, er hat, wir haben, ihr habt, sie haben
- Stem-changing verbs (e→i, e→ie, a→ä): **sprechen** → du sprichst, er spricht / **sehen** → du siehst, er sieht / **fahren** → du fährst, er fährt

### Word order: Verb-Second (V2) rule
The finite verb is ALWAYS in position 2 of a main clause — no matter what comes first.
- ✅ **Heute gehe ich** ins Kino. (not "Heute ich gehe")
- ✅ **Ich gehe heute** ins Kino.

### Articles and gender
Every noun has a gender: **der** (masc.), **die** (fem.), **das** (neut.), **die** (plural).
- Nominative: der Mann, die Frau, das Kind
- Accusative: den Mann, die Frau, das Kind (only masculine changes: der → den)

### Basic connectors
| Connector | Meaning | Word order after it |
|---|---|---|
| und | and | normal (verb 2nd) |
| aber | but | normal (verb 2nd) |
| oder | or | normal (verb 2nd) |
| denn | because/for | normal (verb 2nd) |
| weil | because | verb goes to the **end** |

⚠️ **Watch out:** `weil` sends the verb to the end of the clause, but `denn` does NOT.
- Ich bleibe zu Hause, **weil** es **regnet**. (verb at end)
- Ich bleibe zu Hause, **denn** es **regnet**. (verb 2nd, normal order)

### Capitalization
All nouns are capitalized, always — "das Haus," "die Familie," even mid-sentence.

### Common A1 mistakes
- Forgetting to capitalize nouns
- Wrong verb position after time expressions ("Heute ich bin müde" → "Heute bin ich müde")
- Mixing up der/die/das (learn gender WITH each new noun, not separately)
- Conjugating "sein" and "haben" incorrectly (these are irregular and used constantly)

## A2 — Building Complexity

### Perfekt (conversational past tense)
Formed with **haben/sein** (conjugated) + **past participle** at the end of the clause.
- Ich **habe** ein Buch **gelesen**.
- Ich **bin** nach Berlin **gefahren**.

**haben** or **sein**? Sein is used for movement/change of state verbs (gehen, fahren, kommen, werden, sein, bleiben). Everything else uses haben.

Past participle formation:
- Regular: ge- + stem + -t → **gemacht**, **gespielt**, **gekauft**
- Irregular: ge- + stem(changed) + -en → **gegangen**, **gesehen**, **geschrieben**
- Separable verbs: prefix + ge + stem + t/en → **eingekauft**, **aufgestanden**
- Verbs ending in -ieren: NO ge- → **studiert**, **telefoniert**

### More connectors
| Connector | Meaning | Word order |
|---|---|---|
| deshalb / deswegen | therefore | verb 2nd, but this word counts as position 1 |
| trotzdem | nevertheless | verb 2nd, this word counts as position 1 |
| dann | then | verb 2nd, this word counts as position 1 |
| während | while/whereas | verb to the end (subordinating) |
| obwohl | although | verb to the end (subordinating) |

⚠️ **Watch out:** deshalb/trotzdem/dann trigger inversion — since they occupy position 1, the verb comes right after them, then the subject.
- Es regnet. **Deshalb bleibe ich** zu Hause. (NOT "Deshalb ich bleibe")

### Separable verbs (trennbare Verben)
The prefix detaches and moves to the end of the main clause.
- an**rufen** → Ich **rufe** dich **an**.
- auf**stehen** → Ich **stehe** um 7 Uhr **auf**.
In subordinate clauses (weil, dass, etc.), the verb stays together at the end: ...weil ich dich **anrufe**.

### Modal verbs
| Verb | English meaning |
|---|---|
| können | can / to be able to |
| müssen | must / to have to |
| wollen | to want to |
| sollen | should / to be supposed to |
| dürfen | may / to be allowed to |
| mögen | to like |

Conjugated modal in position 2, main verb as infinitive at the END.
- Ich **muss** heute **arbeiten**. (I have to work today.)
- Er **kann** gut **schwimmen**. (He can swim well.)

### Dative case introduced
Used after certain verbs (helfen = to help, danken = to thank, gefallen = to please/like) and prepositions (mit = with, nach = after/to, bei = at/near, seit = since, von = from/of, zu = to, aus = from/out of).
- Ich helfe **dem Mann** / **der Frau** / **dem Kind**.
- Ich fahre mit **dem Bus**.

### Common A2 mistakes
- Using haben when sein is needed (or vice versa) in Perfekt
- Forgetting to move the separable prefix to the end
- Modal verb + infinitive in wrong position (infinitive must be last)
- Wrong case after prepositions (mit + dative, not accusative)

## B1 — Your Exam Level

### Subordinate clause word order (critical!)
After **weil, dass, ob, wenn, obwohl, damit, als, während, nachdem, bevor, bis, seitdem, falls, sodass** — the finite verb moves to the **very end** of the clause.
- Ich denke, **dass** er **kommt**.
- **Weil** es **regnet**, bleibe ich zu Hause.
- Er ist müde, **obwohl** er viel geschlafen **hat**. (with Perfekt: both parts go to the end, auxiliary last)

⚠️ **Watch out:** this is the single most commonly tested B1 grammar point. Practice it until it's automatic.

### Temporal connectors (bevor, während, nachdem, seitdem, bis, als, wenn)
These all send the verb to the end, but each has a specific meaning and timing that's easy to mix up. Great for adding variety to Erzählung and Bericht tasks instead of always using "und dann."

| Connector | Meaning | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **bevor** | before | Action in the bevor-clause happens AFTER the main clause action |
| **während** | while / during | Two actions happening at the same time (or a contrast — "whereas") |
| **nachdem** | after | Action in the nachdem-clause happens BEFORE the main clause — usually needs Plusquamperfekt in the nachdem-clause if the main clause is in Perfekt/Präteritum |
| **seitdem** | since (that time) | An action/state that started at a point and continues |
| **bis** | until | Marks the endpoint of an action |
| **als** | when (one-time, past) | A single completed event in the past — NOT repeated |
| **wenn** | when / if | Repeated events (any tense) OR conditional ("if") |

Examples:
- **Bevor** ich schlafen **gehe**, putze ich mir die Zähne.
- **Während** ich **koche**, hört mein Bruder Musik. *(simultaneous)*
- **Während** ich gern Tee trinke, bevorzugt sie Kaffee. *(contrast: "whereas")*
- **Nachdem** ich **gegessen hatte**, **bin** ich spazieren **gegangen**. *(Plusquamperfekt + Perfekt — the eating happened first)*
- **Seitdem** ich in Deutschland **wohne**, spreche ich besser Deutsch.
- Ich warte, **bis** der Bus **kommt**.
- **Als** ich zwölf Jahre alt **war**, sind wir umgezogen. *(one-time past event)*
- **Wenn** ich müde **bin**, trinke ich Kaffee. *(repeated / habitual)*
- **Wenn** es morgen **regnet**, bleibe ich zu Hause. *(conditional)*

⚠️ **Watch out — the classic als vs. wenn mistake:** in the past tense, use **als** for a single occasion, **wenn** for something repeated.
- ✅ **Als** ich klein war, hatte ich einen Hund. (one specific childhood, single description)
- ✅ **Wenn** ich als Kind Geburtstag hatte, gab es immer Kuchen. (repeated every year)
- ❌ "Wenn ich klein war, ..." — wrong if it's a single, non-repeated past state.

⚠️ **Watch out — nachdem and tense-backshifting:** since nachdem describes something that already finished before the main action, the nachdem-clause typically needs one tense further back than the main clause:
- Main clause in Perfekt → nachdem-clause in Plusquamperfekt (hatte/war + participle)
- Main clause in Präsens → nachdem-clause in Perfekt

### Relative clauses
Relative pronoun (der/die/das/den/dem/deren...) agrees in **gender and number with the noun**, but its **case depends on its role in the relative clause**. Verb goes to the end.
- Das ist der Mann, **der** hier wohnt. (nominative — subject of relative clause)
- Das ist der Mann, **den** ich kenne. (accusative — object of relative clause)
- Das ist der Mann, **dem** ich geholfen habe. (dative — after helfen)

Comma is ALWAYS required before and after a relative clause.

### Genitive case (start seeing it, mainly written B1)
Used for possession and after prepositions like wegen, trotz, während, außerhalb.
- **wegen des Wetters** (because of the weather)
- **trotz des Regens** (despite the rain)
- Colloquially, "wegen dem Wetter" (dative) is common in speech but **avoid it in writing** — use genitive for exams.

### Konjunktiv II (hypotheticals, politeness)
würde + infinitive, or irregular forms (wäre, hätte, könnte, müsste).
- Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**, **würde** ich reisen.
- Ich **würde** gern nach Berlin fahren.
- Könnten Sie mir helfen? (polite request)

### Passive voice (Passiv) — basic recognition
werden (conjugated) + past participle.
- Das Auto **wird** repariert.
- Das Haus **wurde** 1990 gebaut.

### Adjective endings (weak/mixed/strong declension)
After **der/die/das** (weak): der **große** Mann, die **große** Frau, das **große** Haus
After **ein/eine** (mixed): ein **großer** Mann, eine **große** Frau, ein **großes** Haus
No article (strong): **großer** Mann, **große** Frau, **großes** Haus

⚠️ **Watch out:** this trips up almost everyone. If unsure, default to weak declension pattern (-e/-en) after der/die/das, since that's the safest to remember.

### Connecting words for argumentative writing (Erörterung/Stellungnahme)
| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| Einerseits... andererseits... | On one hand... on the other hand... |
| Meiner Meinung nach | In my opinion |
| Ich bin der Ansicht, dass... | I am of the view that... |
| Ein weiterer Vorteil/Nachteil ist... | Another advantage/disadvantage is... |
| Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen... | To summarize, one can say... |
| Im Gegensatz dazu... | In contrast to that... |

### Präteritum of sein, haben, and modal verbs (use this in writing, not Perfekt!)
Even though Perfekt is the normal spoken past tense, **sein, haben, and modal verbs (können, müssen, wollen, etc.) almost always use Präteritum instead**, even in writing — using their Perfekt forms sounds unnatural/childish.
- ✅ Ich **war** müde. (not "Ich bin müde gewesen")
- ✅ Ich **hatte** keine Zeit. (not "Ich habe keine Zeit gehabt")
- ✅ Ich **konnte** nicht kommen. (not "Ich habe nicht kommen können")

Forms: war/warst/war/waren/wart/waren (sein) · hatte/hattest/hatte/hatten/hattet/hatten (haben) · konnte, musste, wollte, sollte, durfte, mochte (modals — all regular weak endings)

### Possessive articles (mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, ihr/Ihr)
Decline exactly like "ein" — same endings pattern for all four cases.
- Nominativ: **mein** Buch, **meine** Tasche, **mein** Auto
- Akkusativ: **meinen** Bruder, **meine** Tasche, **mein** Auto
- Dativ: **meinem** Bruder, **meiner** Schwester, **meinem** Kind
- Genitiv: **meines** Bruders, **meiner** Schwester, **meines** Kindes

### zu + infinitive constructions (Infinitiv mit zu)
Common in B1 writing to express purpose or manner without a full subordinate clause.
- **um...zu** (in order to): Ich lerne Deutsch, **um** in Deutschland **zu** arbeiten.
- **ohne...zu** (without doing): Er ging weg, **ohne** sich **zu** verabschieden.
- **anstatt...zu** (instead of doing): **Anstatt** fernzusehen, sollte er lernen.
- Simple zu + infinitive after certain verbs: Ich versuche, pünktlich **zu** sein. Es ist wichtig, viel **zu** üben.

⚠️ **Watch out — the "same subject" rule for um...zu:** you can only use **um...zu** when the subject of both clause parts is the SAME person. If the subjects are different, you must use **damit** (a full subordinate clause) instead.
- ✅ **Ich** lerne Deutsch, **um** [**ich**] in Deutschland zu arbeiten. (same subject "ich" in both parts → um...zu is fine, the repeated subject is dropped)
- ✅ **Ich** erkläre es langsam, **damit mein Bruder** es versteht. (different subjects: ich vs. mein Bruder → must use damit, NOT um...zu)
- ❌ "Ich erkläre es langsam, um mein Bruder es zu verstehen." — wrong, because the subjects differ.

⚠️ **Watch out:** with separable verbs, "zu" is inserted BETWEEN the prefix and the stem: an**zu**rufen, auf**zu**stehen. — "Ich habe vergessen, dich an**zu**rufen."

### Verbs that require a zu-infinitive as their object
Certain verbs are typically followed by a "...,zu + infinitive" construction rather than a direct object — very common in B1 writing.
- **anfangen / beginnen** (to begin/start): Er beginnt, Deutsch **zu** lernen.
- **aufhören** (to stop): Sie hört auf, **zu** rauchen.
- **versuchen** (to try): Ich versuche, pünktlich **zu** sein.
- **hoffen** (to hope): Ich hoffe, die Prüfung **zu** bestehen.
- **planen / vorhaben** (to plan/intend): Wir planen, nach Berlin **zu** ziehen.
- **vergessen** (to forget): Er hat vergessen, mich an**zu**rufen.
- **versprechen** (to promise): Ich verspreche, pünktlich **zu** kommen.
- **erlauben / verbieten** (to allow/forbid, + Dativ person): Die Eltern erlauben ihm, spät **zu** kommen.

### Infinitiv Perfekt (perfect infinitive — "to have done") — advanced B1/B2
Used to describe an action that happened BEFORE the action of the main verb, often with verbs of claiming/believing (behaupten, glauben, scheinen).
Formation: **past participle + zu + haben/sein**
- Er behauptet, den Film schon **gesehen zu haben**. (He claims to have already seen the film.)
- Sie scheint gut geschlafen **zu haben**. (She seems to have slept well.)
- Er ist froh, die Prüfung bestanden **zu haben**. (He's glad to have passed the exam.)

This is a more advanced structure — recognizing it in reading is more B1-relevant than needing to produce it fluently in your writing exam, but it's good to have in your toolkit for a stronger essay.

### Punctuation rules (frequently tested)
- Comma **required** before every subordinate clause (weil, dass, wenn, obwohl, relative pronouns)
- Comma **required** on both sides of a relative clause
- Comma optional/wrong before "und"/"oder" in a simple two-item list

### Common B1 mistakes
- Verb not moved to the end in subordinate clauses (biggest point-loss area)
- Wrong case on relative pronouns (confusing "der" as subject vs "den" as object)
- Missing commas before subordinate/relative clauses
- Wrong adjective endings after der/ein
- Using "wegen + Dativ" in formal writing instead of genitive
- Forgetting that in a Perfekt + subordinate clause combo, BOTH verb parts (participle + auxiliary) go to the end, auxiliary last: "..., weil er das Buch gelesen hat."
- Confusing als/wenn in narrative past tense (see Temporal connectors section)
- Wrong case after two-way prepositions — not distinguishing "wohin?" (Akk.) from "wo?" (Dat.)
- Writing "Ich bin müde gewesen" instead of the natural "Ich war müde" (Perfekt of sein/haben/modals in writing)
- Mismatched verb+preposition pairs (e.g. "interessiere mich in" instead of "interessiere mich für")
- Forgetting "zu" between prefix and stem in separable-verb infinitive constructions (vergessen anzurufen, not "zu anrufen")

## B2+ (Quick outlook, not exam-critical tomorrow)

- **Konjunktiv I** for reported speech (indirekte Rede): Er sagte, er **komme** morgen.
- **Nominalization** of verbs for academic style: die Verbesserung, die Entwicklung
- **Extended attributes** (erweiterte Partizipialattribute): der **von der Regierung geplante** Vorschlag
- **Two-part connectors**: sowohl...als auch, weder...noch, nicht nur...sondern auch, je...desto
- More nuanced modal particles (doch, ja, eben, halt) for natural spoken register
- Passive with modal verbs: Das Problem **muss gelöst werden**.

## Tenses — Full Overview (A1 to B1)

The six tenses side by side, since seeing them together is the easiest way to stop mixing them up. All examples use the sentence "I read a book" / "I go to Berlin."

| Tense | English name | When to use it | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Präsens** | Simple present | Right now, habits, general facts, and (with a time word) even future plans | conjugated verb | Ich **lese** ein Buch. / Ich **fahre** morgen nach Berlin. |
| **Perfekt** | Present perfect / conversational past | The normal way to talk about the past in SPEECH — "what happened" | haben/sein (conjugated) + past participle (end) | Ich **habe** ein Buch **gelesen**. / Ich **bin** nach Berlin **gefahren**. |
| **Präteritum** | Simple past | The normal way to talk about the past in WRITING (stories, reports, formal texts) — also always used for sein/haben/modals even in speech | conjugated preterite stem | Ich **las** ein Buch. / Ich **fuhr** nach Berlin. (Ich **war** müde. Ich **hatte** Zeit.) |
| **Plusquamperfekt** | Past perfect ("had done") | An action that happened BEFORE another past action — the "past before the past" | hatte/war (conjugated) + past participle (end) | Ich **hatte** das Buch schon **gelesen**, bevor der Film anfing. |
| **Futur I** | Simple future | Plans/predictions about the future — though Präsens + time word is more common in everyday speech | werden (conjugated) + infinitive (end) | Ich **werde** morgen ein Buch **lesen**. |
| **Konjunktiv II** | Conditional ("would/could/might") | Hypotheticals, wishes, polite requests — see B1 section above | würde + infinitive, or wäre/hätte/könnte | Ich **würde** gern ein Buch **lesen**. Wenn ich Zeit **hätte**... |

### The key distinction that trips people up: Perfekt vs. Präteritum
Both mean "I did X" — the difference is register (spoken vs. written), NOT meaning.
- **Speech / conversation** → use Perfekt: "Ich **habe** gestern ein Buch **gelesen**."
- **Writing / narration / formal reports** → use Präteritum: "Gestern **las** ich ein Buch." or in a story: "Es **war** einmal ein Mädchen, das im Wald **lebte**."
- **Exception that applies to BOTH speech and writing:** sein, haben, and modal verbs (können, müssen, wollen...) almost always use Präteritum (war, hatte, konnte), even in spoken conversation. See the B1 section above for why.

For your B1 writing exam: since Erzählung/Bericht tasks are narrative writing, **Präteritum is actually the more "correct" and natural choice** for regular verbs too, though Perfekt is also accepted and commonly used by learners. Mixing both in one text is fine as long as sein/haben/modals stay in Präteritum.

### Plusquamperfekt — the one people forget exists
Used specifically to show that one past action finished before another past action started — most common after **nachdem** (see Temporal connectors section above).
- **Nachdem** ich **gegessen hatte** (this finished first), **ging** ich spazieren (this happened after).
- Without Plusquamperfekt, the sequence of events becomes unclear: "Nachdem ich aß, ging ich spazieren" sounds like both happened at the same time.

### Futur I — when Germans actually use it
In everyday German, **Präsens + a time word is far more common** than Futur I for talking about the future.
- More natural: Ich **fahre** morgen nach Berlin. (Präsens + "morgen")
- Also correct, more formal/emphatic: Ich **werde** morgen nach Berlin **fahren**.
Futur I is used more for predictions, promises, or assumptions about the present:
- Er **wird** wohl zu Hause **sein**. (He's probably at home — assumption, not really "future")

⚠️ **Watch out:** don't overuse Futur I in your exam writing — Präsens with a time expression (morgen, nächste Woche, bald) sounds more natural for simple future plans.

## Cases — Full Reference (Nominativ, Akkusativ, Dativ, Genitiv)

Cases are introduced gradually (Nom/Akk at A1, Dativ at A2, Genitiv at B1) but you need all four working together for B1 writing. Here's the consolidated picture.

### Definite article (der/die/das) across all four cases
| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | der | die | das | die |
| Akkusativ | **den** | die | das | die |
| Dativ | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den** (+n on noun) |
| Genitiv | **des** (+s/es) | **der** | **des** (+s/es) | **der** |

### Indefinite article (ein/eine) across all four cases
| | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominativ | ein | eine | ein |
| Akkusativ | **einen** | eine | ein |
| Dativ | **einem** | **einer** | **einem** |
| Genitiv | **eines** (+s/es) | **einer** | **eines** (+s/es) |

### What each case actually does
- **Nominativ** — the subject of the sentence ("who/what is doing the action"). **Der Mann** liest.
- **Akkusativ** — the direct object ("who/what receives the action"). Ich sehe **den Mann**.
- **Dativ** — the indirect object ("to/for whom"). Ich gebe **dem Mann** das Buch.
- **Genitiv** — possession ("whose"). Das Auto **des Mannes** ist neu.

### Personal pronouns by case
| English | Nominativ | Akkusativ | Dativ |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / me / to me | ich | mich | mir |
| you / you / to you (informal) | du | dich | dir |
| he-she-it / him-her-it / to him-her-it | er/sie/es | ihn/sie/es | ihm/ihr/ihm |
| we / us / to us | wir | uns | uns |
| you (plural) / you / to you | ihr | euch | euch |
| they-you (formal) / them-you / to them-you | sie/Sie | sie/Sie | ihnen/Ihnen |

⚠️ **Watch out:** mich vs. mir and dich vs. dir are constantly confused. Test yourself: "Er hilft **mir**" (helfen = dative verb) vs. "Er sieht **mich**" (sehen = accusative verb).

### Question words tied to case
- Nominativ: **wer?** (who) / was? (what)
- Akkusativ: **wen?** (whom)
- Dativ: **wem?** (to/for whom)
- Genitiv: **wessen?** (whose)

### Prepositions by case (memorize these groups — heavily tested)
**Always Akkusativ:** durch (through), für (for), gegen (against), ohne (without), um (around/at), entlang (along)
- Ich kaufe ein Geschenk **für meinen Freund**. (I'm buying a gift for my friend.)

**Always Dativ:** aus (from/out of), bei (at/near), mit (with), nach (after/to), seit (since), von (from/of), zu (to), gegenüber (across from/opposite), außer (except)
- Ich fahre **mit dem Bus** **zur Schule**. (I go to school by bus.)

**Always Genitiv:** wegen (because of), trotz (despite), während (during), außerhalb (outside of), innerhalb (within), statt (instead of), aufgrund (due to)
- **Wegen des Regens** bleibe ich zu Hause. (Because of the rain, I'm staying home.)

**Two-way prepositions (Wechselpräpositionen) — Akkusativ OR Dativ:**
an (at/on), auf (on/onto), hinter (behind), in (in/into), neben (next to), über (over/above), unter (under), vor (in front of), zwischen (between)
- **Akkusativ** when there's MOVEMENT/direction (answers "wohin?"): Ich gehe **in die Küche**. (movement into the kitchen)
- **Dativ** when there's LOCATION/no movement (answers "wo?"): Ich bin **in der Küche**. (already there, static)

⚠️ **Watch out:** this is one of the most commonly tested case rules. Ask yourself "wohin?" (destination → Akkusativ) or "wo?" (location → Dativ).
- Ich stelle das Buch **auf den Tisch**. (wohin? → Akkusativ, action of placing)
- Das Buch liegt **auf dem Tisch**. (wo? → Dativ, static position)

### Verbs with fixed prepositions (feste Verb-Präpositionen)
Many verbs pair with a specific preposition + case, and the meaning is often idiomatic (not literal).
- sich freuen **auf** + Akk. (to look forward to) / sich freuen **über** + Akk. (to be happy about, already happened)
- warten **auf** + Akk. (to wait for)
- denken **an** + Akk. (to think of)
- sich interessieren **für** + Akk. (to be interested in)
- Angst haben **vor** + Dat. (to be afraid of)
- träumen **von** + Dat. (to dream of)

⚠️ **Watch out:** these must be memorized as fixed pairs — the preposition doesn't follow logic from English. "Interested in" is "interessiert **an**" (adjective) but "sich interessieren **für**" (verb) — different constructions, easy to conflate.

## Pronouns — Full Reference (A1 to B1)

### Personalpronomen (personal pronouns) — introduced A1, full case forms by A2
| English | Nominativ | Akkusativ | Dativ |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / me / to me | ich | mich | mir |
| you / you / to you | du | dich | dir |
| he / him / to him | er | ihn | ihm |
| she / her / to her | sie (she) | sie | ihr |
| it / it / to it | es | es | ihm |
| we / us / to us | wir | uns | uns |
| you (plural) / you / to you | ihr | euch | euch |
| they / them / to them | sie (they) | sie | ihnen |
| you (formal) / you / to you | Sie (formal) | Sie | Ihnen |

- A1: you mainly just use nominative forms (ich, du, er...) as sentence subjects.
- A2: accusative/dative forms appear with verbs like sehen (+Akk.), helfen (+Dat.), geben (+Dat.+Akk.).
- Ich sehe **ihn**. / Ich helfe **ihr**. / Ich gebe **ihm** das Buch.

### Reflexive pronouns (reflexive Pronomen) — A2/B1
Used with reflexive verbs (sich freuen, sich interessieren, sich waschen...). Identical to personal pronouns EXCEPT 3rd person and Sie, which always use **sich**.

| Person | Reflexive (Akk./Dat.) |
|---|---|
| ich | mich / mir |
| du | dich / dir |
| er/sie/es | **sich** |
| wir | uns |
| ihr | euch |
| sie/Sie | **sich** |

- Ich freue **mich** auf die Reise. (Akk.)
- Ich wasche **mir** die Hände. (Dat. — because "die Hände" is the direct object)

⚠️ **Watch out:** whether the reflexive pronoun is Akkusativ or Dativ depends on whether there's already another direct object in the sentence. If yes (like "die Hände"), the reflexive pronoun becomes Dativ.

### Possessive pronouns/articles (Possessivpronomen) — A1/A2, full declension B1
| German | English meaning |
|---|---|
| mein | my |
| dein | your (informal, singular) |
| sein | his / its |
| ihr | her |
| sein | its |
| unser | our |
| euer | your (informal, plural) |
| ihr / Ihr | their / your (formal) |

Decline exactly like "ein" (see Cases section above for the full table).
- Das ist **mein** Bruder. (This is my brother.) **Meine** Schwester heißt Lisa. (My sister is named Lisa.) Ich rufe **meinen** Bruder an. (I'm calling my brother.)

### Demonstrative pronouns (Demonstrativpronomen) — B1
dieser/diese/dieses (this), jener/jene/jenes (that, rarely used, mostly literary), derselbe/dieselbe/dasselbe (the same)
- Decline like the definite article (der/die/das pattern).
- **Dieser** Film ist besser als **jener** Film. (this film is better than that film — jener sounds formal/old-fashioned; in speech Germans usually say "der andere Film")
- Ich möchte **dasselbe** Buch wie du. (the same book)

Also very common: **der/die/das** used alone (not as article) to mean "this one/that one/he/she/it" — a natural way to avoid repeating a noun.
- Kennst du den Mann? — Ja, **den** kenne ich gut. (den = "him," referring back to der Mann)

### Indefinite pronouns (Indefinitpronomen) — A2/B1
| Pronoun | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| man | one / people (generic "you") | Always Nominativ subject; "man" → "einen" (Akk.) → "einem" (Dat.) |
| jemand | someone | Optional endings: jemand/jemanden/jemandem |
| niemand | no one | Same pattern: niemand/niemanden/niemandem |
| etwas | something | Invariable, doesn't decline |
| nichts | nothing | Invariable, doesn't decline |
| alle | everyone/all | Declines like plural adjective |
| viele / einige / manche | many / some / some (of them) | Declines like plural adjective |
| jeder / jede / jedes | each/every | Declines like "dieser" |

- **Man** muss viel üben, um Deutsch zu lernen.
- Es hat **niemand** angerufen.
- **Jeder** Student muss die Prüfung bestehen.

⚠️ **Watch out:** "man" is impersonal and always stays Nominativ as the subject — never conjugate a verb with "man" like a regular pronoun's other cases as subject. To express "man" in accusative/dative, it changes form: "Das hilft **einem** sehr." (dative form of man)

### Interrogative pronouns (Fragepronomen) — A1 onward
- wer/wen/wem/wessen (who — see Cases section for case forms)
- was (what) — no case forms, invariable
- welcher/welche/welches (which) — declines like "dieser"
- Was für ein(e)...? (what kind of...?) — Was für ein Auto hast du?
